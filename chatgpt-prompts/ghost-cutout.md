# ghost-cutout

For the `halloween_lantern` set — 19 mm motif, 0.4 mm nozzle, so 7% minimum feature. **Inverted polarity** (subject as retained material), which is the variant in `TEMPLATE.md` under "Polarity B".

## History

**Attempt 1 — failed.** Ghost as the hole, face as white shapes: returned two white eyes and a white mouth floating inside the black head. Three loose chips. The rule against it was present but abstract, and lost to the model's prior.

**Attempt 2 — failed differently.** Same polarity, prompt rewritten to name the failure and offer an escape hatch. It correctly *took* the escape hatch and omitted the face — and a faceless ghost is a blob. Recognisable only to someone who had seen the earlier versions.

**The lesson:** unlike a raven or a witch in profile, **a ghost's recognition lives in its face**, so the outer-contour rule cannot rescue it. The face is not decoration to be dropped; it is the subject.

**Attempt 3 — invert.** With the ghost as *material* and the surround as *hole*, the eyes and mouth become holes cut in the ghost, which is topologically free — no chips, no bridges. It also reads better: a dark ghost with **glowing eyes** against a lit window, rather than a glowing ghost with dark eye sockets.

This is the construction abandoned on the flying witch, but the reason it failed there does not apply: she needed thin *interior gaps* (arm from body, hand from broom) at 0.4–0.8 mm. A ghost needs one big mass and three large oval holes.

---

Create a **Halloween ghost** as a **single-piece stencil design**, not an illustration. It should be simple, bold, and instantly recognizable — a classic haunting apparition.

### What the image means

This becomes a **cutout in a flat panel**. There are three regions and each one is a physical thing:

1. **WHITE background**, outside everything — the panel. Solid material.
2. **A single BLACK region** filling most of the image — a **hole**. It is removed, and light shines through it.
3. **A WHITE ghost standing inside that black region** — solid material. This is the ghost itself.
4. **BLACK eyes and mouth inside the white ghost** — small holes cut through the ghost.

Lit from behind, this reads as a **dark ghost silhouetted against a glowing window, with glowing eyes and mouth.** That is the effect to aim for.

### Composition

- The black region is a single **rounded, organic window** — an upright oval or soft blob — filling the image with a small even margin.
- The white ghost stands inside it, large, occupying most of the window.
- **The ghost's trailing robe MUST reach the BOTTOM edge of the black window and merge into the white panel below it.** This is mandatory, not stylistic — it is what physically holds the ghost in place and lets it be built from the bottom up.
- The ghost should also touch the window's edge in **two or three more places** — arm tips, the top of the head, the side of the robe. More contact is better.

### The size it will actually be

The window will be about **19 mm tall — smaller than a thumbnail.**

- The ghost must be recognizable **at that size, by someone who has not been told what it is.**
- **No feature — white or black — may be narrower than 7% of the window's height.**
- Bold and simple. At this size, a plain ghost that reads beats a detailed one that does not.

### Manufacturing rules — these outrank the artwork

**1. The ghost must be ONE connected white shape**, joined to the white panel outside the window at the bottom and at two or more other points. No part of the ghost may be a separate piece.

**2. No white may be isolated.** Every white pixel must have a path to the white background without crossing black. An isolated white shape is a loose chip that falls out.

**3. Nothing may begin in mid-air.** The panel builds up in horizontal layers from the bottom, so every part of the ghost must connect **downward** to more of the ghost, or to the panel. No floating wisp, no arm that starts detached, no shape whose lowest point has only black beneath it.

**4. Eyes and mouth are BLACK holes fully inside the white ghost.** Large and simple — two oval eyes and one oval mouth is ideal. Each at least 7% of the window's height across. **They must not touch the ghost's outline** — keep a clear margin of white all the way around each one.

**5. Blunt, not ragged.** The robe should end in **a few large rounded lobes** — never fine tatters, wisps, tendrils or spikes. Arms should be thick and simple: no curled claws, no separated fingers.

**6. Favour sweeping curves, steep slopes and tapered forms.** Avoid long shallow near-horizontal edges on the undersides of white shapes.

### Style

- Professional Halloween stencil; bold vector graphic.
- Pure black and pure white only. No greys, no shading, no gradients, no textures, no lighting, no perspective.
- Crisp hard edges.
- No border, no frame, no background elements beyond the three regions described.

### Check before you answer

State your answer to each:

1. Is the ghost one single connected white shape?
2. Does its robe reach the bottom edge of the black window and merge into the white panel below?
3. How many other places does the ghost touch the window's edge?
4. Is any white area isolated inside the black? Name it.
5. Are the eyes and mouth black holes, fully surrounded by white, not touching the ghost's outline?
6. Is every feature at least 7% of the height in width?
7. Would someone shown only this, at thumbnail size, say "ghost"?

If any check fails, fix it and check again. **When appearance conflicts with manufacturability, always favour manufacturability.**
